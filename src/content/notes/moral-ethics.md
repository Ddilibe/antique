It’s funny how quickly people take the moral high ground when someone else acts on the exact impulse they had right up until it causes a problem.

I was stuck in a traffic jam the other day. A few commuters urged our driver to bypass the line and form an unofficial second lane. Fortunately, our driver refused. Another driver wasn't as patient; they pulled out to force a second lane, which immediately turned gridlock into total chaos. We ended up losing an extra hour on top of the time we’d already spent sitting there.

Naturally, the very commuters who had been encouraging the idea began condemning the driver who actually did it.

Immanuel Kant argued that before taking action, we should follow a maxim that could be applied universally. As someone watching both sides of this hypocrisy unfold, I was left dumbfounded.

So what was their universal rule here? *How does this benefit me—and only me?*
