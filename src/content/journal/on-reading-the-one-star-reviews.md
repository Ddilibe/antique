---
title: 'On Reading the One-Star Reviews'
description: 'Why the reviews we avoid — the one- and two-star ones — often teach us more than the praise we already agree with.'
date: 2026-11-12
tags: ['criticism', 'perspective', 'attention']
issue: 7
---

When you watch a really good movie, you naturally tend to look up its reviews. On a scale of one to ten, you're more likely to gravitate towards the seven-, eight-, nine-, and ten-star reviews. However, the problem with these reviews is that they often become generic and bland. They start to sound alike, and you eventually stop getting the fresh perspectives or positive reinforcement you were looking for.

But if you really want to rediscover and deepen your appreciation for a movie, try reading the one- and two-star reviews as well. They might reveal things you overlooked, introduce perspectives you hadn't considered, and highlight flaws you might otherwise have missed. Looking at both ends of the spectrum gives you a more balanced understanding of the movie.

I believe this principle extends far beyond movies; it applies to life in general. It's important to expose yourself to both positive and negative perspectives. Positive feedback can encourage you, while criticism can help you recognise your blind spots and identify areas for improvement. Ultimately, what matters is how you interpret and respond to both.

That's the point I'm trying to make: just as exploring both positive and negative movie reviews can deepen your appreciation of a film, considering different perspectives in life can broaden your understanding of the world and of yourself.
